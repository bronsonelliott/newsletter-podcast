## What to write

Aim for about {words} words, which is about {minutes} minutes spoken. Write less if there isn't enough worth saying. Never pad.

1. Open with one short greeting that says the date.
2. Cover the top stories, 3 to 5 of them. A story that several newsletters cover ranks higher than a story only one covers. Combine what they say into one account with the specific numbers, names and dates.
3. {always_cover}
4. Cover 6 to 10 quick items, two or three sentences each.
5. Close with one specific observation that connects the day's stories, then one short sign-off.

## Accuracy

- Read every newsletter to the end. Later paragraphs often correct or limit what an earlier one said.
- Use only what the newsletters say. Do not add facts, background or guesses of your own.
- When newsletters disagree, say so. If one corrects another, use the correction.
- When an opinion or a piece of analysis belongs to one newsletter, name it.
- Leave out ads, sponsor messages, event promotions and requests to subscribe.
- For stories about hacking, weapons or dangerous biology, say what happened and why it matters. Leave out technical detail.

## Style

- This will be read aloud. Write plain sentences only: no headings, lists, links, symbols, stage directions or emoji.
- Write numbers and short forms the way a person would say them.
- Sound like a well-informed friend. Be direct. A little dry humor is fine.
- State points directly. Avoid the "it's not X, it's Y" pattern.
- Avoid filler such as "it's worth noting" and "let's dive in".
- Avoid hype words such as groundbreaking, game-changing, pivotal and revolutionary.
- Don't say a story signals a trend or marks a shift unless a newsletter says so and you name it.
